# Strategy briefs on deals in The Wall Street Journal

Author: Saathvik Maheshuni

For Economics and Strategy at NYU Tandon (fall 2025), I wrote short "What's the Point?" briefs on business news. Each one takes a Wall Street Journal article and sums up its main point in one sentence. Then it covers the plan of action, the situation, the objective and outcome, and how general the lesson is, meaning which other companies are doing the same thing.

| Brief | WSJ article | The point |
|---|---|---|
| [Compass buys Anywhere](briefs/01_compass_acquires_anywhere.pdf) (Oct 6, 2025) | "Brokerage Giant Compass Agrees to Acquire Rival Anywhere for $1.6 Billion" (Sept 22, 2025) | In a weak market, buying a rival is a way to grow, cut costs and get stronger instead of going it alone. |
| [JPMorgan's security investments](briefs/02_jpmorgan_security_and_resiliency.pdf) (Oct 20, 2025) | "JPMorgan to Invest $10 Billion in U.S. Companies Critical to National Security" (Oct 13, 2025) | When supply chains and national security look at risk, companies put money into protecting key industries instead of only chasing the highest profit. |
| [Google's AI build-out](briefs/03_google_ai_infrastructure.pdf) (Nov 10, 2025) | "Google to Invest $24 Billion in AI in U.S., India" (Oct 14, 2025) | Companies that invest early in new technology infrastructure can lock in a strong position in future markets. |

Some of the numbers in the briefs:

- Compass gives 1.436 of its shares for each Anywhere share, so Compass shareholders end up owning about 78% of the combined company. The two companies expect $225 million a year in cost savings.
- JPMorgan's $10 billion of its own money is part of a $1.5 trillion, 10-year plan across four areas: defense and aerospace, energy independence, advanced manufacturing, and emerging technologies.
- Google's plan is $9 billion for data centers in South Carolina through 2027 and $15 billion for AI data centers and hubs in India over the next five years.

The briefs put the articles in my own words. The articles themselves are behind the WSJ paywall, so they aren't included.
